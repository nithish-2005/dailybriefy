Indian Don't need to play GTA 6, we live it everyday

Published on 20 October 2026

The world is going gaga about Grand Theft Auto 6. The game has been in the making for over a decade. Millions of copies have been sold within days and millions of gamers are spending sleepless nights within the Vice City getting their cheap thrills. Some in India, too, are excited about it, about the possibility of hitting the roads in Vice City, about the lawlessness that pervades the town, about the promise of loot and chaos. Well, they don’t need to. I fail to understand the appeal of GTA 6 for Indians. We live GTA life on a daily basis. Ask people in Patna, for example. In fact, I would suggest that you don’t need to spend big bucks on getting the game and then wasting your hours playing it. Someone on social media calculated the cost.
They said get a console. That would be some Rs 50,000-60,000 for either the PS5 or the Xbox One. Then the cost of the game. That is at least Rs 5,999 for the standard variant. Add to it other sundry costs. Say a carton of coke and a box full of potato chips GTA is a game built around a simple concept: You have to survive and thrive in a big bad city and the only way for you to do this is by breaking rules and by squeezing the NPCs hard. It is a life without rules that you live in GTA, the one where exploiting NPCs is not just fun but necessary. You need to rob the rest of the world around you, loot it, kill people, drive erratically without any regard for rules. If you follow rules, traffic or otherwise, you will quickly find yourself at a loss. GTA is a game that forces you to create chaos and mayhem, and the more you do it the better are your chances of success
It is the same life we are forced to live in India. If you follow rules here, traffic or otherwise, the Thar boys will run over you. We, too, live a life where thriving and surviving means ignoring rules, squeezing our fellow humans, and walking through the roads as if we own them. If you try to follow rules, try to be kinder, try to do the right thing, you will be a roadkill, metaphorically speaking.
GTA, there is a city called Vice City. In India, all our cities are vice cities. And surviving them requires the same ruthlessness that your hero in GTA needs. For the alphas, which is what a playing character is in a video game like GTA, life is an adventure. Exactly how it is for our alphas — think a successful gangster or a successful politician. For NPCs, life is all about existing for the benefit of alphas, always being at the risk of robbery, exploitation, harassment and dying. That is most of us in India. We too exist for the benefit of our alphas, for giving bribes to them, for doing favors for them, or simply entertaining them with our lack of lathi. After all, jiski lathi uski bhains. It is as true for life in GTA as it is for life in India.
