Earth's messenger reaches Mercury: BepiColombo captured by planet's gravity

BepiColombo has achieved orbital capture around Mercury after its journey through the inner Solar System. The milestone clears the way for two orbiters to begin complementary scientific investigations.

Published on 10 September 2026

Humanity's latest messenger to Mercury has reached a historic milestone. The European-Japanese BepiColombo spacecraft has achieved orbital capture around the Solar System's smallest planet, marking a major step towards its long-awaited scientific mission to study the mysterious world. In an update shared on social media, the BepiColombo team confirmed that the spacecraft had re-established contact with ground controllers and that flight dynamics teams at the European Space Operations Centre (ESOC) had verified its successful orbital capture around Mercury.


WHAT IS THE BEPICOLOMBO MISSION? Launched on October 20, 2018, BepiColombo is a joint mission of the European Space Agency (ESA) and the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA). It is designed to conduct the most comprehensive investigation yet of Mercury, a planet that remains poorly understood despite being Earth's closest planetary neighbour in terms of orbital proximity to the Sun
